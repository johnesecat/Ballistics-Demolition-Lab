# Projectile Destruction Physics — Design

Date: 2026-11-26

## Intent

The round is currently a sprite plus a pre-baked wreck timeline: on contact the
solver emits 57 scripted frames (parabola + spin + a `crumple` ramp) and the
renderer solves a *local* pixel lattice purely for looks. Nothing the round does
after contact is decided by the round's own mass, and the round never carries on
through a plate once it has stopped.

This replaces that with one physical model:

- The round is a component body: **copper jacket**, **steel penetrator**,
  **copper driving band**, **steel boat-tail**. Each component has its own mass,
  strength, ductility, conductance and heat-softening behaviour.
- Contact runs a **world-space soft-body solve** (mass nodes + axial/shear/
  bending constraints, plastic yield, ductile fracture, wall + ground
  collision). Every post-impact frame is produced by that solve; there is no
  authored motion.
- When the jacket is damaged past its limit the **steel penetrator breaks free**
  and keeps going with its own hardness, which bores the metal in front of it
  instead of stopping on the face.
- **Heat is a real state**: contact friction and jet/burn phases put thermal
  energy into metal. Hot metal softens and warps like a red-hot blade on an
  anvil — but only while its transient heat is above the working threshold, after
  which the new shape is frozen in. Applies to every metal (armor, steel, copper).

## Goals

1. A shot's wreck is generated by the solver from component masses, impact
   energy, face material and the round's own lattice damage — never a timeline.
2. The jacket sheds; the penetrator separates and crosses layers it could not
   cross as a whole round.
3. Fragments that come off the round keep their own mass, spin and velocity, and
   a hot metal chip deforms plastically where it lands.
4. Zoomed in, the round reads as an x-ray diagram: one coloured box per
   component with that component's name inside it.
5. Hot metal (walls and fragments) bends for a short thermal window and then
   holds the bent shape.

## Non-goals

- No change to the campaign structure, HUD, or the pre-computed damage outcome
  model. The wreck remains presentation: damage is decided before playback, so
  pause / skip / speed can never alter a result.
- No new third-party physics engine. Matter.js stays as the decorative debris
  layer only; the round is solved by the project's own deterministic code.

## Approaches considered

**A. Bake the soft body into `ShotResult` frames** (run the solve once at fire
time, ship every cell position). Deterministic and simple to replay, but it puts
~150k numbers per shot into the replay payload (bad on iPhone) and is not
"generated in real time".

**B. Solve the soft body in the renderer, stepped by replay time** (chosen).
`ShotResult.wreck` becomes a small `WreckState` (pose, momentum, mass, seed,
components). `ProjectileWreck` in `sim/soft-body.ts` builds the lattice in world
space and is stepped forward from replay time with the shot's own walls and
ground. Deterministic, tiny payload, and pause/speed only change how fast the
same fixed-step solve advances.

**C. Reuse `ShellImpact` and just add world translation.** Cheap, but the
existing solver has a fixed impact plane and no wall/ground collision, so the
casing could not tumble, land or slide — the exact "canned animation" problem.

## The model

### Components and mass

`SHELL_BASE` cells now carry a `component`. `COMPONENTS` holds each component's
label, projectile material (copper / steel) and diagram colour. Mass is
`area × density × seeded imperfection`; the penetrator fraction of the round mass
is derived from those cells, so a heavier round has a heavier core without any
per-ammo table.

### Soft body

Nodes from the sampled lattice, springs on the 4-neighbourhood plus both
diagonals. Per step:

1. contact heat from the face (friction), conduction along intact links, cooling;
2. spring forces with heat softening; past yield the rest length creeps
   (permanent warp) and the link accumulates damage until it snaps;
3. integrate with gravity and air drag;
4. collide every node against wall polygons (nearest edge / inside test, local
   AABB rejection) and the ground, with restitution and friction that scales
   with the node's mass;
5. separate disconnected clusters: a copper chip flies off with the velocity it
   had, a steel cluster keeps its momentum and stays recognisable.

Hot metal yields at a lower strain (`soften`) and warps further, so a glowing
casing folds instead of snapping — and when it cools the folded shape stays.

### Penetration and separation

`crossLayer` keeps a per-round shape quality. The jacket has quality 1 (it
mushrooms and transfers energy); the steel penetrator has a much lower effective
resistance because a hard ogive does not deform. The jacket sheds when its
integrity falls past `RELEASE_INTEGRITY` after a crossing, or when the round runs
out of speed on a layer — in both cases the core retries the layer it just
failed on with the better quality. If the core still cannot pay, it pancakes and
the wreck is built from the core alone.

### Thermal warping window

`settleHotMetal(state)` runs a short transient field (heat that decays to zero
inside the window) through `deformHeatedWalls`. The permanent scorch is
unchanged, but the *motion* stops when the transient heat does, which is the
"red-hot on the anvil, then rigid" behaviour. Brick and concrete never warp.

## Final state (2026-11-26)

The two-model split described above is gone. `sim/shell-pixels.ts` and
`sim/soft-body.ts` were deleted; `sim/body-sim.ts` is the only round and
`sim/wall-pixels.ts` is the only wall it touches. Penetration is the body
carving pixels it can pay for, so there is no layer cost table deciding the
shot any more. Playback draws a baked `BodyTrack` (60 Hz, Int16 quarter-units +
byte heat), so the phone never steps the body.

Measured (`npm run bench:body`, desktop node, phone = ×4 estimate): replay frame
0.71 ms median (2.8 ms phone, inside a 60 Hz budget); whole-shot resolve 19.5 ms
median (78 ms phone, once per shot, guarded at 80 ms); track 24 frames / 18 KiB.
`BODY_DT` stays 1/240 s: 1/180 s is unstable with `SPRING_K = 22000`.

Balance moved into the physics: core damage is the energy the round still has on
arrival, and core health is ~2.5× the old values. `npm run balance` reports
0/15 destroy-core batteries clearable in one shot (was 14/15 before), all
clearing in 2–3.

Verification is now `npm test` (raster, collision incl. one-body glancing
contact, body, projectile/art, save, deform), `npm run bench:body`,
`npm run balance`, `npm run build`, plus a live AH-02 landscape check.

## Final pass: preview parity, exact pixel holes, soft-body wall

Three gaps remained after the one-body rewrite and are now closed.

**The preview is the shot.** `previewShot()` calls
`simulateShotWalls(def, snapshot, input, { damage: false })` and reads every
claim from that result: path = the solver's flight frames, `willHitCore` =
`coreDamage > 0`, `ricochets` = the body's own `bounces`, stop point = the
body's resting centre. The old preview's `crossLayer()` arithmetic and its
scripted `* 0.63` ricochet (which teleported the round off the face) are
deleted, so the guide cannot show a deflection the shot will not make. Wall
damage and the follow-on phases are suppressed for the probe; the body, the
raster and the contact rule are identical. The guide is throttled to 120 ms
during a drag because it is a real body solve now, and the mission-start
opening-line search uses `probeAim()`, a flight-only probe that is never shown.

**The hole is the carve.** `subtractPixelCells()` in `fracture.ts` removes the
exact union of the 2-unit cells the body detached. Rows are merged into
rectangles, then subtracted from the convex wall polygons with
`subtractPixelRect()` (four exact pieces per rectangle), and touching
rectangles are merged back. `fractureWallBand()` is no longer used for a
projectile bore; the old oriented band cut a rotated rectangle through
material the round never touched. A bolted wall keeps its anchor unless the
bore takes more than 45% of its area, so a plate is no longer dropped by its
first hole.

**The wall is a soft body.** `WallPixels` rigs the solid pixels within 22
units of contact as a mass-spring lattice: mass = density × area, axial and
diagonal links with stiffness from the material's strength, plastic flow past
`yieldStrain`, and link tear. Every contact calls `push()`, which moves the
touched pixel and its connected neighbours by a mass-, speed- and
material-weighted depth; `stepSoftBody()` integrates only the awake nodes.
After the shot, `deformationAt()` samples the permanent displacement field and
`applyCarves()` bends the surviving wall vertices around the hole, so the rim
keeps the shape the pixels were pushed into. Detached cells become the chips.

Cost: the final bench is 17.7 ms median resolve / 70.8 ms ×4 phone estimate;
repeat runs range 17–22 ms and 71–84 ms, inside the 80 ms guard. The first
soft-body cut was 20.9 ms / 83.6 ms; the 22-unit patch and awake-only
integrator brought it back. `npm run bench:body` exits 0.

The campaign validator now filters candidates with `probeAim()` (a full body
solve per aim is ~18 ms, too slow for a 75k-point sweep) and simulates the 80
core-line candidates nearest the core per shell and mode. It cleared all
nine destroy-core batteries in 2–3 APDS shots, all three relay batteries in
one wide-jet, and all three expose-core batteries in one shell (two HE
blasts, one quake). Full log: `artifacts/verify/validate-final.log`.
